For scheduling vulnerabilities, I took inspiration from Operating System CPU scheduling and process states.

Similar to how an operating system manages processes, I modeled vulnerabilities through different states, such as Ready, Running, Waiting, Completed, and Error.

The scheduler was responsible for selecting vulnerabilities that were ready for processing and routing them to the remediation workflow.

If a vulnerability required human approval, it could move into a Waiting state instead of being treated as continuously active. Once approval or feedback was received, the vulnerability could continue through the appropriate workflow.

This gave the system a structured way to manage vulnerabilities that were simultaneously at different stages of remediation.
